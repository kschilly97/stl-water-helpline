Do these first, safely

While you wait for help:

  1. Stay out of water near outlets, cords or the electrical panel. Cut power only if you can reach the breaker safely.
  2. Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or supply line is leaking.
  3. Photograph and video everything before you move it, for insurance.
  4. Move valuables up and out. Don't use a household vacuum on standing water.

Why speed matters

Drying should start within 24–48 hours

That's the window commonly cited for limiting mold growth. In St. Louis, the usual causes are basement seepage and sump pump failures during spring storms, frozen pipes in winter cold snaps, failed water heater and washer lines, and sewer backups in older neighborhoods.

Before the provider arrives

  1. Call your homeowners insurance to open a claim.
  2. Keep receipts for anything you buy or any emergency repairs.
  3. Lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks to prevent staining.
  4. Open windows if it's dry outside, to help air circulate.

If anyone's life or safety is at risk, call 911.

FAQ

Common questions

Are you a restoration company?

No. STL Water Help Line is a free referral service. We connect you with independent local providers who do the work, and we may be paid for that referral.

Does it cost anything to call?

The call and referral are free. If you hire a provider, it gives you its own estimate and pricing.

How fast should water damage be dried?

Ideally drying starts within 24 to 48 hours, the window commonly cited for limiting mold growth.

Will my homeowners insurance cover it?

It depends on your policy and the cause. Sudden events like a burst pipe are often covered, while outside flooding and gradual leaks often are not. Check with your insurer.

How do I check a provider before hiring?

Ask for proof of insurance and any licensing your municipality requires, get a written estimate, and check independent reviews. Each provider is responsible for its own credentials and work.
